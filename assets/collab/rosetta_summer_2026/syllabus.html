Rosetta Stone of Neural Mass Models — internship syllabus for Lucia & Claudia

One shared week of AI, neural networks, and LLMs, calibrated to a strong mathematics background. Then a guided implementation of the model ladder — from harmonic oscillators to next-generation mean fields — ending in two public artefacts.

Tutors: Francesca Castaldo & Giulio Ruffini · Neuroelectrics / BCOM · June 2026

Start: June 2026 (Week 1 shared) Duration: Lucia 4 wks · Claudia 8 wks Location: Neuroelectrics, Barcelona Shared folder: Google Drive

“Understanding is the ability to see one thing in many ways.”

— R. P. Feynman, opening of the Rosetta Stone paper

This internship has two parts. Week 1 is a shared crash course in AI, neural networks, large language models, and the practical use of Claude as a research tool — calibrated to a strong pure-mathematics / mathematical-physics background. From Week 2 onward you each work on the Rosetta Stone of Neural Mass Models paper: reading it carefully, then implementing the model ladder it describes and producing a public-facing artefact. Lucia's headline deliverable is a BCOM-style website that is the paper; Claudia's is a deeper implementation that extends Lucia's scaffold.

1 · Goals & learning outcomes

By the end of the internship you should be able to:

  1. Explain, at the level of a mathematician new to the field, what neural networks and large language models are, what they optimise, and what their structural priors are.
  2. Use Claude (and one other frontier coding assistant of your choice) as a working collaborator — reading code, designing experiments, drafting documentation — without losing your own grasp of the problem.
  3. Read the Rosetta Stone paper closely enough to reconstruct its derivation ladder: harmonic oscillator → Stuart–Landau → Wilson–Cowan → NMM1 (Jansen–Rit / laminar) → NMM2 (Montbrió–Pazó–Roxin).
  4. Implement at least three models from that ladder in clean, tested Python, with reproducible figures, and embed them in a small open-source repository.
  5. Produce a public-facing artefact (Lucia: a BCOM-style website mirroring the paper; Claudia: an extended interactive companion).

2 · Logistics & working rhythm

Cadence

  • Daily standup (15 min, asynchronous Slack thread or a short call): yesterday / today / blockers.
  • Weekly sync (60 min, with Francesca and Giulio): walk through code, demo figures, plan the next week.
  • One paper presentation per intern at the end of the programme (Claudia gets a mid-internship demo as well).

Tools

  • Python 3.11+, numpy, scipy, matplotlib, jupyter, jitcdde / numba for ODE/DDE work.
  • Git + GitHub; one repo per intern, linked to a shared organisation.
  • Claude (desktop or web) as the primary AI collaborator; a second assistant of your choice for comparison.
  • Quarto or Astro for the website (Lucia's call), styled to match the bcom.one palette: Lively Blue #002878 and Coral #FF6657.
House rule on AI use

You may use Claude for anything — but every line of code or prose you submit, you must be able to explain and defend. If you can't reconstruct the reasoning without the assistant, the work isn't yours yet. Treat the AI like a very fast, occasionally wrong, very patient collaborator.

3 · Phase 1 · Week 1 — AI / NN / LLM crash course shared

The aim of Week 1 is not to make you ML engineers — it is to give you the conceptual handles you need so that, when an LLM proposes code or an architecture, you can judge it. We assume strong calculus, linear algebra, probability, and (for Lucia) measure theory. We will not assume any prior Python ML.

Day 1 — What is a neural network?

Read

  • Nielsen, Neural Networks and Deep Learning, Chapters 1–2. ~2 h
  • LeCun, Bengio & Hinton (2015), Deep learning, Nature 521. overview

Watch

  • 3Blue1Brown, Neural Networks (4-video series). ~1 h

Do

  • Set up Python, NumPy, Jupyter. Implement a 2-layer perceptron from scratch on MNIST in pure NumPy — no PyTorch yet. Aim for >95% test accuracy.
Day 2 — Backprop, optimisation, and why depth works

Read

  • Nielsen, Chapter 2 (backprop derivation) and Chapter 3 (cross-entropy, regularisation).
  • Optional, for the mathematically curious: Roberts, Yaida & Hanin, The Principles of Deep Learning Theory, §1 only. depth

Watch

  • Karpathy, The spelled-out intro to neural networks and backpropagation: building micrograd. 2 h 25 m — essential

Do

  • Follow Karpathy along; produce your own micrograd.py. Then re-implement Day 1's MLP using it.
Day 3 — From convnets to transformers

Read

  • Vaswani et al. (2017), Attention is All You Need. Skim the experiments, read §3 carefully. classic
  • Phuong & Hutter (2022), Formal Algorithms for Transformers. a mathematician's entry point

Watch

  • 3Blue1Brown, But what is a GPT? Visual intro to transformers.

Do

  • On paper: write out a single self-attention head as a linear-algebraic expression. Verify dimensions for a toy batch.
Day 4 — What LLMs actually are

Watch

  • Giulio Ruffini — How Claude Works (LLMs talk). Slides: Google Slides. internal-perspective; required
  • Karpathy, [1hr Talk] Intro to Large Language Models.
  • Karpathy, Let's build GPT: from scratch, in code, spelled out. 2 h — work along

Read

  • Anthropic, Mapping the mind of a large language model (Scaling Monosemanticity, blog summary).
Anchor concepts from the slides

Predict-next-token = compression = world model. A predictor that good must compress; a compressor that good must model. The frozen weights are an implicit world-model — that's what makes the prompt stack worth designing.

Two parts working together. A trained model is frozen weights (what it knows) plus your markdown — CLAUDE.md, SKILL.md, your message — read fresh every session (how it behaves today).

The model is stateless. Memory lives in the application, not the model. Every turn, the host reconstructs the full context and resends it.

Do

  • Build a tiny character-level transformer on Shakespeare following Karpathy's nanoGPT. ≤200 lines, ≤30 min training on CPU.
Day 5 — Using Claude as a research collaborator

Courses (Anthropic)

  • Anthropic Courses index — work through, in order:
    1. API fundamentals
    2. Prompt engineering interactive tutorial
    3. Tool use
  • Prompt-engineering documentation — reference.

Do

  • Pick one numerical experiment from Phase 2 Day 1 reading (e.g. simulate a damped harmonic oscillator with forcing) and implement it twice: once writing the code yourself, once pair-programming with Claude. Compare the two sessions in a one-page note: what was faster, what was wrong, what you learnt about prompting.
  • Set up a project-level CLAUDE.md in your internship repo — start small (style preferences, vocabulary, do-not-do rules) and grow it as the weeks go on.

End-of-week mini-deliverable

  • A 2-page memo (Markdown, in the shared Drive folder) titled "What I now think a neural network / LLM is" — your own words, your own analogies, mathematician-to-mathematician. Francesca and Giulio will read it before Week 2 kickoff.

4 · Phase 2 · Rosetta Stone implementation

The paper (resources/TN0366___Rosetta_Stone_of_Neural_models/main.tex) walks a ladder of neural mass models, each rung introduced first as an isolated node, then under forcing, then in coupled networks. Phase 2 asks you to walk that same ladder in code.

RungModelKey idea§
0Undamped & damped harmonic oscillatorPhase-only and damped linear resonance2
1Stuart–Landau (SL)Cubic nonlinearity → Hopf limit cycle3
—Synapses & transfer functionalsLinear filters + sigmoid I/O4
2Wilson–Cowan (WILCO)E–I push-pull rate model; Hopf via sigmoid5
3NMM1 (Jansen–Rit, laminar)Second-order synapses + static sigmoid → PSP dynamics6
4NMM2 (Montbrió–Pazó–Roxin)Exact mean-field from QIF spiking networks7

4.1 · Lucia 4 wks · 3 on Rosetta

Mission · Lucia

Read the paper carefully and build a website that is the paper — a navigable, illustrated, runnable companion to the Rosetta Stone, in BCOM style (Lively Blue / Coral, Lato, the patterns used across bcom.one). Aim for the standard set by Distill and the Transformer Circuits threads. The site must include three working model simulations (the minimum spanning set, see Week 4).

Week 2 — Read the paper, set up the site

Read

  • Rosetta Stone paper §1 (Introduction) and §2 (Linear response and phase-only limits) — slowly, with pen and paper. Reproduce every equation in §2.
  • Cabral, Hlinka et al., Functional connectivity dynamically evolves on multiple time-scales over a static structural connectome — for context on why anyone cares about whole-brain coupled oscillators.

Do

  • Implement the damped harmonic oscillator with sinusoidal forcing in Python. Reproduce the resonance-curve figure from §2. Commit to a fresh repo rosetta-stone-site.
  • Choose the website stack (recommended: Quarto for the math-heavy text, with MathJax + matplotlib figures exported as SVG; or Astro + KaTeX if you want full control of layout).
  • Stand up the empty site skeleton in BCOM style: one page per paper section, blue header + coral accent, footer, deploy to GitHub Pages.

Milestone

  • Friday demo: skeleton site online; first interactive figure embedded.
Week 3 — Stuart–Landau and Wilson–Cowan

Read

  • Rosetta Stone §3 (Stuart–Landau) and §5 (Wilson–Cowan), and the §4 interlude on synapses and transfer functionals.
  • Wilson & Cowan (1972), Excitatory and inhibitory interactions in localized populations of model neurons.

Do

  • Implement single-node SL: integrate the complex Stuart–Landau equation, reproduce the Hopf bifurcation diagram as you sweep μ.
  • Implement single-node Wilson–Cowan: reproduce the limit cycle in the (E,I) plane for canonical parameters.
  • Add both to the site as interactive figures (sliders for μ, gains, time constants — vanilla JS or Observable embeds are fine).

Milestone

  • Friday demo: §2, §3 and §5 of the paper are all live pages with at least one runnable figure each.
Week 4 — Coupled networks + write-up + presentation

Do

  • Extend SL or WILCO to a coupled network on a small toy connectome (e.g. 6 Kuramoto-style nodes, or a 2-node E–I motif). Reproduce the "coupling" subsection figure of the corresponding model.
  • Write the §6/§7 pages of the site as careful expositions (you do not need to implement NMM1/NMM2 — handing the baton to Claudia — but the pages must summarise the derivations correctly).
  • Polish: typography, citations (BibTeX → bibliography page), an "About this site" page crediting Castaldo, Ruffini, de Palma Aristides, Clusella, Garcia-Ojalvo.
  • Prepare a 20-minute final presentation: what you read, what you built, what you would do next.

Final deliverables (Lucia)

  1. Public website (GitHub Pages) covering all six paper sections, in BCOM style, with three runnable simulations (DHO, SL, WILCO).
  2. Source repo, well-documented README, MIT or CC-BY licence.
  3. Final presentation + a short reflection memo (1 page).

4.2 · Claudia 8 wks · 7 on Rosetta

Mission · Claudia

Take the scaffold Lucia builds and push the implementations down the ladder, all the way to NMM2. Your deliverable is a Python package nmmlab with clean implementations of every model in the paper, plus a notebook gallery reproducing the key figures, woven into Lucia's site as an interactive companion.

Weeks 2–3 — Foundations: read the whole paper once + DHO, SL

Read

  • The full Rosetta Stone paper, end-to-end, lightly. Write a 2-page "map of the paper" showing which sections you understand fully, partially, or not at all.
  • Pikovsky, Rosenblum & Kurths, Synchronization, Chapters 1–3 (in the resources folder).

Do

  • Set up nmmlab: poetry/uv-managed Python package, pytest from day 1, type hints, GitHub Actions CI.
  • Implement nmmlab.dho (damped harmonic oscillator) and nmmlab.sl (Stuart–Landau) with a shared Simulator interface.
  • Reproduce Figs. 2.x and 3.x of the paper as matplotlib notebooks. Pixel-comparable, not exact.

Milestone (end of W3)

  • Two models, full test coverage, two reproduced figures, CI green.
Weeks 4–5 — Wilson–Cowan + the synapse/transfer interlude

Read

  • Rosetta Stone §4 (synapses) and §5 (WILCO) carefully — these are the conceptual bridge to NMM1/NMM2.
  • Cowan, Neuman & van Drongelen (2016), Wilson–Cowan equations for neocortical dynamics.

Do

  • Implement nmmlab.synapse: first- and second-order linear filters, with the operator formalism from §4.1.
  • Implement nmmlab.wilco: single-node WILCO with sigmoidal transfer; reproduce the limit cycle and Hopf-locus figure.
  • Extend to coupled WILCO on a toy 4-node network with delays (use jitcdde).

Milestone (end of W5)

  • Mid-internship presentation (45 min) to Francesca and Giulio: what the ladder looks like in code so far, what surprised you, what you're worried about for NMM2.
Weeks 6–7 — NMM1 (Jansen–Rit, laminar)

Read

  • Rosetta Stone §6 (NMM1) in full.
  • Jansen & Rit (1995), Electroencephalogram and visual evoked potential generation in a mathematical model of coupled cortical columns.
  • Sanchez-Todo, Bastos, Lopez-Sola, Mercadal et al. (2023), A physical neural mass model framework for the analysis of oscillatory generators from laminar electrophysiological recordings — the LaNMM paper.

Do

  • Implement nmmlab.nmm1: Jansen–Rit canonical (E, P, I) populations with second-order PSP filters and static sigmoid. Reproduce alpha-rhythm output for default parameters.
  • Stretch: implement the laminar variant from Sanchez-Todo et al. — even a stripped-down two-layer version.
Week 8 — NMM2 (next-generation mean field) + final write-up

Read

  • Rosetta Stone §7 (NMM2) carefully — this is the hardest section.
  • Montbrió, Pazó & Roxin (2015), Macroscopic description for networks of spiking neurons.
  • Coombes & Byrne (2019), Next generation neural mass models.

Do

  • Implement nmmlab.nmm2: the Montbrió–Pazó–Roxin (r, v) mean-field equations for a QIF network. Reproduce one figure from the paper (e.g. the spiking-vs-mean-field comparison in §7).
  • Wire the gallery into Lucia's site (one notebook per model, exported to HTML; or use Quarto's native Python execution).
  • Final presentation (45 min), final reflection memo (2 pages): what would NMM3 need to look like, given what you have built?

Final deliverables (Claudia)

  1. nmmlab Python package on PyPI (or at minimum installable from GitHub) with all six rungs of the ladder.
  2. Notebook gallery reproducing the key figures, embedded in Lucia's website.
  3. Final presentation + reflection memo.
